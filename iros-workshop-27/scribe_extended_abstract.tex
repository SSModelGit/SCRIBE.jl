\begin{abstract}
Distributed exploration requires more than independent robots learning
similar models. Agents must be able to combine what they have learned without
treating correlated beliefs as independent evidence, and they must continue
learning when the complete communication network is unavailable. This work
presents SCRIBE, Signal-aware Collaboration for Reliable Information-based
Estimation, as a distributed model-learning architecture for these conditions.
SCRIBE represents an environment through a fixed set of Gaussian scalar-field
bases, reducing online field learning to linear estimation of a shared
coefficient vector. The resulting information-filter update separates prior
knowledge from newly observed innovations. Metropolis-Hastings Markov Chain
message passing propagates innovations through the currently reachable
communication graph, while decentralized covariance intersection fuses priors
whose cross-correlations are unknown. Agents can therefore update locally,
exchange fixed-dimensional model states whenever communication is available,
and repeatedly fuse models without artificially increasing certainty.
Simulation studies show that SCRIBE recovers centralized filtering under
connected communication and preserves consistent estimates through network
separation and reconnection. A final study uses the same posterior as the
modeling backend for information-based planning. SCRIBE consequently provides
a shared probabilistic representation through which a robot team can continue
learning, coordinate future sampling, and exploit intermittent communication
during one-shot ocean and space exploration missions.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

An autonomous robot exploring the deep ocean or deep space must make
scientific decisions before a complete description of the environment is
available. A team can distribute this task over several robots, but only if
measurements gathered by one agent can inform the decisions of another. The
team consequently requires a shared probabilistic model of the environment,
not simply a collection of locally accurate maps. Disagreement in the model
mean directs agents toward inconsistent scientific targets, while
overconfidence in the model covariance causes the team to disregard regions
that remain poorly observed.

The communication assumptions required by many distributed learning methods
are poorly matched to this setting. Sharing all observations causes storage,
modeling, and communication costs to grow with the mission. Averaging model
parameters is compact, but provides no probabilistic account of duplicated
information. If a belief returns to its originating agent through the
communication graph, treating that belief as independent evidence increases
information without introducing a new observation
\cite{chen2002estimation}. Network separation makes the problem more severe:
agents continue gathering measurements, their priors diverge, and their
cross-correlations are generally unavailable when communication returns.

SCRIBE addresses this problem by using a model structure for which estimation
and communication are both linear. The approach combines three mechanisms.
First, Gaussian scalar-field bases provide a fixed-dimensional approximation
of a spatial process \cite{jadaliha2012environmental,cui2015mutual}. Second, an
Information Kalman Filter expresses each new observation as an additive
innovation that can be incorporated locally and exchanged independently of the
complete observation history. Third, a hybrid decentralized update applies
Metropolis-Hastings Markov Chain (MHMC) consensus to new innovations and
Covariance Intersection (CI) to priors with unknown cross-correlation
\cite{xiao2005scheme,wang2012distributed,tamjidi2021unifying}. This distinction
allows an agent to continue filtering without waiting for every member of the
team and allows models to be fused repeatedly without misrepresenting their
probability measure.

The contribution is therefore not another field basis or a new exploration
reward. SCRIBE provides a shared epistemic state suitable for distributed
exploration: locally updateable, asynchronously communicable, conservative
under unknown correlations, and fixed in dimension over the mission. The
experiments evaluate the integrity of this shared state under connected,
disconnected, and reconnected networks, then demonstrate its use by an
information-based planner.

\section{Distributed Environment Model}

\subsection{Linear Field and Information State}

Let $\mathcal{F}(x,k)$ be a scalar process at location $x\in\Omega$ and
discrete time $k$. SCRIBE represents the field as a linear composition of
$n_{\phi}$ Gaussian scalar-field bases,
\begin{align}
    \mathcal{F}(x,k)
    &=\sum_{i=1}^{n_{\phi}}\psi_i(x)\phi_i(k)
      =\bm{\psi}^{T}(x)\bm{\phi}(k),\\
    \psi_i(x)
    &=\frac{1}{\tau_i}
      \exp\left(-\frac{\lVert x-\mu_i\rVert_2^2}{\sigma_i^2}\right).
    \label{eq:field-model}
\end{align}
The basis parameters are shared before deployment. Online model learning is
therefore reduced to estimation of the stochastic coefficient vector
$\bm{\phi}(k)$. With linear field dynamics and additive Gaussian process and
measurement noise,
\begin{align}
    \bm{\phi}(k+1) &= \bm{A}\bm{\phi}(k)+\bm{w}(k),\\
    \bm{z}_j(k) &= \bm{H}_j(k)\bm{\phi}(k)+\bm{v}_j(k),
    \label{eq:state-observation}
\end{align}
where $\bm{H}_j(k)$ evaluates the bases at the sampling locations of agent
$j$. This construction supplies the flexibility of a fixed-basis GP
approximation while retaining the update structure of a linear estimator.
Distributed GP methods instead commonly exchange observations, inducing
points, or locally trained model approximations
\cite{allamraju2017communication}.

Each agent maintains the information vector
$\bm{y}_j=\bm{P}_j^{-1}\hat{\bm{\phi}}_j$ and information matrix
$\bm{Y}_j=\bm{P}_j^{-1}$. A local measurement supplies the additive
innovations
\begin{align}
    \delta\bm{i}_j &=
    \bm{H}_j^T\bm{R}_j^{-1}\bm{z}_j,\\
    \delta\bm{I}_j &=
    \bm{H}_j^T\bm{R}_j^{-1}\bm{H}_j.
    \label{eq:information-innovation}
\end{align}
The agent can form its prior, gather measurements, and update these
innovations without access to a central node. The state communicated during
consensus contains one vector and one matrix determined by $n_{\phi}$; its
size does not grow with the accumulated observation history.

\subsection{MHMC Innovation Consensus}

New innovations and previously accumulated priors must be treated
differently. Innovations at the current filtering epoch represent newly
observed evidence. SCRIBE propagates them through local neighbor exchange. For
either consensus variable
$\bm{\xi}\in\{\overline{\delta\bm{i}},
\overline{\delta\bm{I}}\}$, agent $j$ applies
\begin{align}
    \gamma_{jm}^{l}
    &=\frac{1}{1+\max\left(
        \lvert\mathcal{N}_j^{l}\rvert,
        \lvert\mathcal{N}_m^{l}\rvert
      \right)},\\
    \bm{\xi}_j^{l+1}
    &=\bm{\xi}_j^{l}
      +\sum_{m\in\mathcal{N}_j^{l}}
      \gamma_{jm}^{l}\left(\bm{\xi}_m^{l}-\bm{\xi}_j^{l}\right).
    \label{eq:mhmc}
\end{align}
These MHMC weights recover the average innovation over each reachable
component without a fusion center or a prescribed global topology. Each
iteration depends only on the current local variable and messages received
from immediate neighbors. An agent need not suspend local sampling until the
entire team becomes reachable. Connected agents can converge internally,
disconnected components can continue gathering information, and later
messages can propagate what was learned during the separation.

\subsection{Consistent Fusion of Divergent Priors}

The priors held after independent operation cannot be summed or averaged as
though they arose from independent data. They may contain common information
from any earlier exchange, and the cross-correlation required for optimal
Kalman fusion is no longer known. SCRIBE instead applies decentralized CI.
At agent $j$, weights over the currently available neighborhood are selected
according to
\begin{equation}
    \bm{\omega}^{*}
    =\underset{\bm{\omega}\geq 0,\ \bm{1}^{T}\bm{\omega}=1}
      {\operatorname{arg\,min}}\ 
      \mathcal{J}\left[
      \left(\sum_{m}\omega_m\bm{Y}_m^{-}\right)^{-1}
      \right],
    \label{eq:ci-objective}
\end{equation}
and the fused prior is
\begin{equation}
    \left[\widetilde{\bm{y}}_j,\widetilde{\bm{Y}}_j\right]
    =\sum_m\omega_m^{*}
      \left[\bm{y}_m^{-},\bm{Y}_m^{-}\right].
    \label{eq:ci-fusion}
\end{equation}
CI does not assign the same evidential meaning to model agreement that a
Kalman update assigns to a new measurement. It instead returns a consistent
covariance for all admissible unknown cross-correlations. After CI reconciles
the priors, the MHMC average of the genuinely new innovations is applied,
\begin{align}
    \bm{y}_j^{+}
    &=\widetilde{\bm{y}}_j+n_c\overline{\delta\bm{i}}_j,\\
    \bm{Y}_j^{+}
    &=\widetilde{\bm{Y}}_j+n_c\overline{\delta\bm{I}}_j,
    \label{eq:hybrid-update}
\end{align}
where $n_c$ is the number of agents represented by the consensus. In a
connected network with common priors, the update recovers centralized
information filtering. After disconnection, the same form preserves
consistency while incorporating measurements gathered by the reconnected
agents. The separation of priors from innovations is what permits repeated
and asynchronous model fusion without double-counting evidence.

\section{Consequences for Exploration}

Each agent recovers coefficient moments from its information state and obtains
the posterior mean and covariance of the field at arbitrary locations. This
shared posterior serves three roles during exploration. It communicates what
the team currently believes, identifies where the field remains uncertain,
and provides a common basis for assigning future measurements. In particular,
the mutual information of a scalar observation at $x$ is
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{I}\left(\bm{\phi};z_x\mid\mathcal{D}\right)
    =\frac{1}{2}\log\left(
        1+\frac{\bm{H}_x\bm{P}\bm{H}_x^T}{R}
    \right).
    \label{eq:mutual-information}
\end{equation}
The quantity is useful only when $\bm{P}$ is a defensible covariance.
Artificial confidence from duplicated model fusion directly suppresses the
estimated value of future samples and can cause a distributed team to abandon
unobserved regions. SCRIBE's consistency is consequently an exploration
capability, not merely a filtering property.

The architecture does not require SCRIBE to define the planning objective.
Each robot or team-level planner can combine the model-derived information
reward with vehicle motion, safety, energy, or return constraints. The
fixed-dimensional belief can be used locally while disconnected and exchanged
when a peer becomes reachable. This permits a mission to interleave sampling,
planning, and communication rather than reserving model fusion for globally
synchronized communication windows.

\section{Experimental Evaluation}

Four studies evaluate the claims relevant to distributed exploration. The
distributed experiments use four agents, matched sampling sites and
measurements across methods, and 30 Monte Carlo trials. Metrics include field
RMSE, interval coverage, inter-agent consensus error, distance to a
centralized posterior, covariance conservatism, and communicated payload.

The first study maintains a connected graph. SCRIBE recovers the centralized
information-filter posterior to numerical precision, validating MHMC
innovation consensus and the scaling in
\eqref{eq:hybrid-update}. Independent agents learn only from local
measurements and retain large prediction differences.

The second study separates the graph while every agent continues sampling,
then reconnects the components. As summarized in
Fig.~\ref{fig:split-reconnect}, SCRIBE allows beliefs to diverge during
separation and restores inter-agent agreement following reconnection. The
reconciled result remains close to the centralized oracle. Naive information
summation transmits fewer bytes, but its interval coverage collapses because
correlated priors are misappropriated as new information. Independent
filtering avoids this overconfidence but cannot use measurements collected by
the other component. The experiment therefore isolates the benefit of the
hybrid method: CI protects the probability measure, while MHMC disseminates
new evidence.

The third study repeats separation and reconnection with a true field outside
the span of the selected bases. Centralized filtering and SCRIBE exhibit the
expected representation error, while SCRIBE remains close to the centralized
posterior and preserves comparable uncertainty coverage. The result separates
model misspecification from fusion error. Distributed consensus cannot correct
an inadequate representation, but the communication process does not
introduce an additional uncontrolled source of confidence.

The final study uses SCRIBE as the modeling backend for VulcanJ, an
information-based Monte Carlo tree-search planner. The planner selects one
sampling location at a time using integrated variance reduction and
conditions the same model state used during planning. The true field contains
broad periodic structure and local positive and negative phenomena whose
locations do not coincide with the model bases. Across 108 measurements,
field RMSE decreases from $0.698$ to $0.316$ and integrated posterior variance
decreases from $3.315$ to $0.056$. This experiment demonstrates the immediate
use of the SCRIBE information state for planning. The same interface permits
each agent to plan locally, then improve its belief through opportunistic
fusion with reachable teammates.

\section{Scope and Conclusion}

The current evaluation assumes known vehicle locations, calibrated Gaussian
sensor noise, a common basis dictionary, and defined filtering epochs.
Delayed messages, uncertain team membership, time-varying fields, and coupled
communication and motion require further study. These limitations do not
change the central distinction between prior fusion and innovation consensus.

SCRIBE enables a robot team to maintain more than coincident model means. It
provides a shared and probabilistically consistent statement of what the team
has learned. The linear information update allows new measurements to be
included locally and communicated without replaying the observation history.
MHMC message passing distributes those innovations over the available graph,
while decentralized CI permits independently updated models to be fused after
communication returns. Agents can therefore continue exploring while
disconnected and exploit each communication opportunity without falsely
claiming information that was never observed. This capacity is central to
distributed ocean and space exploration missions in which no agent can assume
continuous contact with its teammates or with a human operator.
