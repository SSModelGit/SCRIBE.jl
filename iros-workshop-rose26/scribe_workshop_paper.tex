\begin{abstract}
SCRIBE addresses distributed environment-model learning under limited
communication while preserving accurate, mutually consistent estimates of the
field and its uncertainty. It represents spatial models as fixed-dimensional
linear states and introduces a fusion process that lets agents repeatedly
assimilate any available peer's model without full-team participation.
Information Kalman filtering adds new evidence,
Covariance Intersection reconciles divergent priors, and
Metropolis--Hastings consensus propagates innovations using only local neighbor
information. The resulting model update is lightweight, asynchronous, and
consistent under intermittent communication. In 96
missions with 3--10 agents, final
normalized error is $0.405$--$0.409$, against $0.582$--$1.227$ without
communication. Across 432 planning trials, median fusion through ten agents
remains below $6.2$~ms and $24$~kB per update. Independent Vulcan planners
achieve pooled posterior variance within 3.2\% of ideal centralized sharing,
demonstrating effective exploration through communication loss.
\end{abstract}

\begin{keywords}
autonomous exploration, distributed estimation, informative path planning,
intermittent communication, multi-robot systems
\end{keywords}

\section{Introduction}

Autonomous exploration uses an uncertain environment model to decide which
observations are most valuable \cite{hwang2019auv,cui2015mutual}. This is
particularly important in the deep ocean, where vehicles may localize plumes,
characterize cold seeps, or follow chemical gradients from sparse data
\cite{vrolijk2021using}. Teams expand coverage, but acoustic links are
intermittent, bandwidth-limited, and range-dependent \cite{hwang2019auv}.
Robots must therefore learn and plan in isolation, then incorporate peer
knowledge whenever contact resumes.

Distributing environment-model learning is non-trivial: each robot requires a
locally useful model, yet team models must remain consistent in prediction and
confidence. Intermittent links cause robots to accumulate different evidence
and their learned states to diverge. Existing methods either exchange
observations and relearn from the
collective history, whose cost grows with mission duration and sensor dimension
\cite{viseras2016decentralized,allamraju2017communication}, or exchange and
average compact model parameters, which can count correlated information more
than once and overstate certainty \cite{chen2002estimation}. The fundamental
problem is not communication reduction alone, but guaranteeing an accurate,
consistently calibrated shared model when only subsets of the team can
participate in each update.

Distributed GP updates \cite{jang2020multi}, finite-dimensional regression
\cite{pillonetto2019distributed}, and model-based planning
\cite{jang2021fully,nguyen2025spatially,kim2023distributed} establish the value
of collaborative learning. SCRIBE contributes a model-learning and fusion
mechanism that remains consistent under intermittent communication. A shared
GSF or EOF basis first recasts the spatial model as a fixed-dimensional linear
coefficient state. SCRIBE then interleaves an Information Kalman Filter, whose
observation updates are additive, with distributed Covariance Intersection,
which reconciles divergent priors without their unknown cross-correlations
\cite{chen2002estimation,wang2012distributed}. Metropolis--Hastings consensus
propagates the new information using only current neighbor degrees
\cite{xiao2005scheme}. This hybrid construction \cite{tamjidi2021unifying}
allows any available agents to repeatedly fuse their learned fields without
loss of consistency, a global graph, or a synchronized team-wide update.

\section{Distributed Environment Modeling}

\subsection{Fixed-Dimensional Information State}

When no collection of earlier field maps is available, SCRIBE represents a
scalar field with $n_\phi$ Gaussian scalar-field (GSF) basis functions
\cite{pillonetto2019distributed,jadaliha2012environmental},
\begin{align}
    \mathcal{F}(x,k)
    &=\bm{\psi}^{T}(x)\bm{\phi}(k),
    &
    \psi_i(x)
    &=\tau_i^{-1}
      e^{-\lVert x-\mu_i\rVert_2^2/\sigma_i^2}.
    \label{eq:field-model}
\end{align}
The centers $\mu_i$ and widths $\sigma_i$ place overlapping functions across
the region; the coefficients $\phi_i(k)$ determine how strongly each function
appears in the field at time $k$.

When a prior environmental record is available, a mean-centered, spatially
weighted singular value decomposition instead yields empirical orthogonal
function (EOF) modes \cite{young2024greedy}. If $\bm E_r$ contains the $r$
retained modes at the stored feature locations, the field vector is
\begin{equation}
    \bm f(k)=\overline{\bm f}+\bm E_r\bm\phi(k),
    \label{eq:eof-field-model}
\end{equation}
where the prior mean $\overline{\bm f}$ and modes remain fixed while
$\bm\phi(k)$ describes the current field. GSFs suit a poorly known environment,
while EOFs compress spatial structure present in earlier data.

Online estimation differs only in constructing the observation matrix. GSF
rows are $\bm\psi^T(x)$ at the sample locations. For EOFs, interpolation
$\bm L_{\bm X_j}$ maps the stored grid to those locations, giving
$\bm H_j=\bm L_{\bm X_j}\bm E_r$ and centered data
$\bm z'_j=\bm z_j-\bm L_{\bm X_j}\overline{\bm f}$. GSF data require no mean
subtraction. Both satisfy $\bm z'_j=\bm H_j\bm\phi+\bm v_j$. Agent $j$ stores
$\bm Y_j=\bm P_j^{-1}$ and $\bm y_j=\bm Y_j\hat{\bm\phi}_j$; a measurement adds
\begin{align}
    \delta\bm{I}_j
    &=\bm{H}_j^T\bm{R}_j^{-1}\bm{H}_j,
    &
    \delta\bm{i}_j
    &=\bm{H}_j^T\bm{R}_j^{-1}\bm{z}'_j.
    \label{eq:information-innovation}
\end{align}
SCRIBE exchanges an $O(n_\phi^2)$ state independent of mission duration,
rather than an $O(kd_z)$ observation history. From
$\hat{\bm\phi}_j=\bm Y_j^{-1}\bm y_j$, GSFs predict
$\bm\psi^T(x)\hat{\bm\phi}_j$; EOFs use
$\bm L_{\bm X}(\overline{\bm f}+\bm E_r\hat{\bm\phi}_j)$.

\subsection{Fusion under an Unstable Network}

Agents' priors may contain shared observations, so adding them can count the
same evidence repeatedly. Let
$\mathcal N_j^{\ell+}=\mathcal N_j^\ell\cup\{j\}$ and
$\Delta_j^\ell=\{\bm\omega\geq0:\sum_{m\in\mathcal N_j^{\ell+}}\omega_m=1\}$.
Distributed CI fuses
these correlated priors without requiring their cross-correlations:
\begin{align}
    \bm\omega^\star
    &=\underset{\bm\omega\in\Delta_j^\ell}{\arg\min}\;
      J\!\left[\left(\sum_{m\in\mathcal N_j^{\ell+}}
      \omega_m\bm Y_m^\ell\right)^{-1}\right],
    \label{eq:ci-weights}\\
    (\widetilde{\bm y}_j^{\ell+1},\widetilde{\bm Y}_j^{\ell+1})
    &=\sum_{m\in\mathcal N_j^{\ell+}}\omega_m^\star
      (\bm y_m^\ell,\bm Y_m^\ell).
    \label{eq:ci-update}
\end{align}
Current measurement innovations are new evidence and are instead averaged by
MHMC. For $\delta\bm s_j\in\{\delta\bm i_j,\delta\bm I_j\}$,
\begin{align}
    \gamma_{jm}^\ell
    &=\frac{1}{1+\max(|\mathcal N_j^\ell|,|\mathcal N_m^\ell|)},
    \label{eq:mhmc-weights}\\
    \delta\bm s_j^{\ell+1}
    &=\delta\bm s_j^\ell+\sum_{m\in\mathcal N_j^\ell}
      \gamma_{jm}^\ell(\delta\bm s_m^\ell-\delta\bm s_j^\ell).
    \label{eq:mhmc-update}
\end{align}
The weights require only current neighbor degrees. After consensus within a
component of size $n_c$,
\begin{align}
    \bm{y}_j^{+}
    &=\widetilde{\bm{y}}_j+n_c\overline{\delta\bm{i}}_j,
    &
    \bm{Y}_j^{+}
    &=\widetilde{\bm{Y}}_j+n_c\overline{\delta\bm{I}}_j.
    \label{eq:hybrid-update}
\end{align}
CI therefore preserves consistent confidence while MHMC distributes new
observations. Fusion begins whenever a link appears, without a global graph or
common schedule.

\noindent\textit{Computational scaling.}
For basis size $b$, messages are $\Theta(b^2)$ and assimilating $q_j$ samples
is $O(q_jb^2)$. An MHMC round is $O(d_jb^2)$ locally and $O(Eb^2)$ fleet-wide;
CI is $O(J_{\mathrm{CI}}(d_jb^2+b^3))$. Hence bounded-degree fleets scale as
$O(Nb^2)$ per round, versus $O(N^2b^2)$ for complete graphs. Fixed-graph
convergence is $O(\log(e_0/\epsilon)/(1-\rho))$; for changing links, it depends
on the link process.

\section{Experiments and Results}

\noindent\textit{Consistency after reconnection.}
Four agents first follow matched paths, isolating fusion from planning. Across
30 trials with disjoint links, a middle-third blackout, and reconnection,
SCRIBE reaches RMSE $0.117\pm0.011$, close to centralized sharing
($0.101\pm0.012$) and CI ($0.118\pm0.012$), but below IKF without CI
($0.288\pm0.020$) and no communication ($0.814\pm0.018$). Reconnection
therefore does not treat correlated priors as independent evidence.
Fig.~\ref{fig:field-reconstruction} also shows that EOFs trained on prior Ram
Head ROMS output \cite{zhang2025fine} retain the dominant held-out structure;
the two-mode estimate reaches RMSE $0.049$ after 300 samples.

\noindent\textit{Fusion without scheduled communication.}
Agents then sense continuously for 8178 steps in adjacent regions and exchange
models only at proximity encounters. Every team size in
Fig.~\ref{fig:asynchronous-fusion} converges near centralized NRMSE
($0.404$--$0.409$), versus $0.582$--$1.227$ without communication. The
six-agent trajectories converge from distinct initial errors; residual spatial
smoothing is the two-mode representation floor, not an asynchronous-fusion
failure.

\noindent\textit{Operational scaling.}
Across 432 trials with 2--10 agents, Fig.~\ref{fig:agent-scaling} separates
estimation quality from fusion cost. Posterior variance remains stable, while
network mixing governs consensus rounds, time, and payload. The worst case,
the intermittent moderate-degree graph at ten agents, requires about 26
rounds, $6.2$~ms, and $24$~kB per update. Model quality therefore persists as
the fleet grows; connectivity determines the operational cost.

\noindent\textit{Effect on information-guided exploration.}
Four independent Vulcan planners \cite{ayton2018vulcan} explore for 36 rounds
with distance-limited links and a twelve-round blackout in
Fig.~\ref{fig:distributed-performance}. SCRIBE has the lowest final distributed
error and, like CI, contracts disagreement after reconnection; IKF remains high
because it does not reconcile correlated priors. SCRIBE's final RMSE
($0.523\pm0.060$) approaches centralized sharing ($0.458\pm0.052$), improves
on CI ($0.564\pm0.084$), and halves the no-communication error
($1.041\pm0.053$).

Pooling each regime's samples isolates path quality. SCRIBE nearly matches
centralized pooled RMSE ($0.455$ versus $0.458$) and integrated variance
($0.0488$ versus $0.0473$), the latter within 3.2\%. Consistent fusion thus
recovers almost all information gathered under ideal sharing, without joint
planning or team-wide communication.

\section{Conclusion}

SCRIBE provides consistent, fixed-dimensional fusion through intermittent
communication. Tests through ten agents approach centralized information
collection, while $O(b^2)$ messages keep local models usable during blackouts.
The planner-independent update supports distributed GP and MCTS systems
\cite{jang2021fully} or Multi-Agent Vulcan \cite{olkin2024multi} across remote
missions.
